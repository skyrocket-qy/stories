At first glance, an Amazon box, just a vehicle for delivery, seems to play a negligible role in the world.

However, these boxes often go far beyond their original purpose.

They become pet homes, all-purpose containers, Halloween costumes, signs for yard sales or protests, and even kids’ toys.

They reduce waste, spark creativity, and show surprising versatility.

“Because they don’t restrict how you use them”

Now, imagine that,

if Amazon designed their boxes to only open one way.

Download the Medium app
Or made them with flimsy material that falls apart after using.

All those reuse possibilities vanish, even if someone had a great idea for them.

The value doesn’t just lie in what the box was made for — it lies in how well it fits unexpected uses.

“The best inventions start by solving a problem — but the real value appears when their interface fits more than one use.”

Like the heart of the Go idiom:
“Return structs, not interfaces.”

When you return an interface, you’re often restricting how others can use what you built — much like a fragile Amazon box that breaks if you try to repurpose it.

But when you return a struct:

You give the caller something concrete.

They can use it, embed it, extend it.

By designing with reusability and flexiblility, you leave room for creativity, not constraint.

Just like a good old cardboard box.
